# E3: The context of a system

Stage E3 of `plan2.md`, in milestone 5, "Additional architecture context". The owner asked
on 2026-11-25 what is worth adding about a whole system, so that a design made from the
architecture's files alone goes wrong less often: "obligations, compliance, architecture
preferences?" Two trials and the earlier research were weighed (`research/context/`,
`research/context2/`, `research/system-context/`, all out of Git), and the main session and
Astra each proposed a form. This page is the one that came of it. Built by the main session.

**After it** a system's record holds what is true of the system and its team and that no
other record says. `seefore check` holds its form, and the system's page shows it.

## The key

In `systems/<id>.yaml`, beside `name` and `description`:

    context:
      - must: Fans' personal data stays in the EU.
        why: The contracts with promoters require it. It is why there is one cloud region.
      - must: Nothing is deployed in the 30 minutes before an on-sale.
        why: An on-sale brings 60,000 fans within five minutes.
      - prefer: Work that need not be waited on goes through a topic on the Event stream.
        why: Nothing waits for Tickets. Chosen in 2023 over retries in Orders.

| Key | What it holds | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `context` | The lines of the system's context. Left out when there is nothing to say | 1 to 12 lines, none twice |
| `must` | A line that binds: a design that goes against it is wrong | A sentence or two on one line, 1 to 300 characters |
| `prefer` | A line that decides between designs that are allowed. It may yield, and its `why` says when | The same |
| `why` | Its reason, and where it comes from: a contract, a decision, somebody who can vouch | The same. Needed on every line |

A line has exactly one of `must` and `prefer`. A text folded with `>-` is one line.

## What a line is

- **The key says how strong the line is, and nothing says its topic.** A law, a contract, a
  condition of a release and a budget that is fixed are all `must`. An architectural
  preference is `prefer`. What comes first when goals pull apart is one `prefer` line that
  says the order.
- **A line is a limit with its reason, not a description.** "It sells in three countries"
  limits nothing, and in the trial it sent two designs the wrong way. The `why` is the guard:
  a line that only describes has no reason to give.
- **Nothing is written where there is nothing to say.** No heading, no "not said": in the
  trial an empty heading made designers fixate on it.
- **Somebody must be able to vouch for a line.** A person says it; no tool fills it in from
  what is written elsewhere.
- **An outside system may have lines too**: what its owner's schedule or contract fixes.

## What the check says

It holds the form, and no more. It cannot tell a true line from a stale one.

| Fault | Rule | The problem says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Both `must` and `prefer` on a line, or neither | `context-line` | Which line, and to keep one, or which to write |
| The same sentence on two lines | `context-line` | Both lines, and to keep one |
| No `why`; a key that is not known | Those of one file (B1) | The key, and the three keys a line has |
| More than 12 lines; a text over 300 characters or on two lines; a list with nothing in it | Those of one file (B1) | The limit |

A value of the wrong kind is now told as one problem, not two, for every record.

## On the page

A system's page has a section "Context", under its facts: one row a line, with "Must" or
"Prefer", the sentence, and under it its reason. With no context there is no section.

## What is out

- **Lists by topic** (obligations, releases, limits, preferences): the key says what a
  design needs to know, and an empty list misleads.
- **A third strength, for a line somebody may waive**: for now that is a `must` whose words
  name who says yes.
- **A record of its own for the context**: a folder, an id and a link to its system more.
- **A target or a load**: it stays with the flow that promises it, or its part.
- **Who may reach what**: a rule the check judges. **A decision**: its own part, later.
- **Assumptions and risks as lists**: in the trial most were how a part behaves, and one
  risk in 37 was lacked twice.
- **Judging a design against a line.** A person or a reviewer does that.

## For the owner to approve

| # | The choice | Built | The other way |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | One list, or lists by topic | One list | Lists by topic, each a reminder to the owner |
| 2 | Strengths | Two: `must`, `prefer` | Three, with one for a line somebody may waive |
| 3 | Where it lives | On the system's record | A record of its own in a new folder |
| 4 | The limits | 12 lines, 300 characters a text | Other numbers: both are guesses |

## Tests

Three in the model: a system with context reads and holds its lines; each fault of a line is
one problem at its line; no line twice, twelve at most, a text on one line. One of the
schema. One in the browser: the section on the system's page.

## The try

The lines of Boxwick (9) and Vellumoak (8), taken from each system's own description, read
with no problem, and each system's page shows them. What the trials say of their worth: a
limit that is written down is respected more often, 21 times in 27 against 13. They do not
show fewer wrong designs overall.
