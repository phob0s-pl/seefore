# B4: Architecture diagram

Stage B4 of `plan2.md`: the Architecture page shows the model as a diagram. The owner
described it on 2026-11-23 and answered two questions; this page writes that down. "arch" is
the owner's earlier tool, whose look the page has since B3. Builder: Opus.

## What you can do after it

Open the page and see the whole model as boxes and arrows. In the top bar choose Systems,
Containers or Components to see it at another depth. Move the diagram and zoom it with the
mouse, or with the three buttons: zoom in, zoom out, and fit.

## What is in

- **A selection in the top bar**, beside the two arrows: Systems, Containers, Components, in
  the look of arch's level switch. Systems is chosen at the start. The choice stands in the
  address, so the back and the forward arrow step through the choices.
- **What each depth draws.** Always the whole model: every person, every system, and no
  owner and no technology.

  | Depth | What is opened |
  | --- | --- |
  | Systems | Nothing. Every system is one box |
  | Containers | Each system that has parts is a frame, with its apps, its stores and its pieces of infrastructure inside |
  | Components | The same, and each app that has components, or keeps a store, is a frame too, with them inside |

- **Arrows.** One arrow for each pair of drawn boxes and each direction, with no words. An
  end is drawn at the deepest thing that is drawn: at the Systems depth a connection to a
  component ends at that component's system. A connection whose two ends fall into one drawn
  box is not drawn. What a connection passes through, its `via`, is not drawn.
- **The layout is ELK's**, run in a worker of the browser with its plain layered layout, frames
  inside frames included. Nothing is tuned.
- **The drawing is React Flow's**, with boxes and frames in the look of arch's diagram. Arrows
  follow the routes that ELK gives.
- **The field of the diagram** is arch's hatched, rounded field. Its lower edge is level with
  the lower edge of the sidebar.
- **Three buttons at the lower left of the field**, as in arch: zoom in, zoom out, fit. The
  diagram is fitted when it opens and after each change of depth.
- **What a view shows is worked out in `src/model/`**, by one function with no browser in it:
  the records and a depth go in; boxes, what is inside what, and arrows come out. Each arrow
  says which connections it stands for.
- **`@xyflow/react` and `elkjs` are added**, as A3 names them.

## What is out

The owner asked for no further logic in the diagram yet.

- **A click on a box or an arrow**, and a panel that reads it.
- **Opening one box from the diagram**, and one element with its neighbours.
- **Names on arrows**, and the route of a connection.
- **Any tuning of the layout**: an order kept after an edit, a split of a long column, places
  set by hand, a small map of the whole.
- **Search and the list of records**: B5.

## Tests, about ten

What a view shows, on small models written inline: each depth; an end drawn at the deepest
drawn thing; several connections as one arrow that names them all; no arrow inside one box;
a store an app keeps; a system with no parts. And one test in a real browser: the built page,
served by the command, draws a known box and a known arrow. It drives the Chrome that is
installed, needs no new dependency, and stops what it starts.

## The try

On the private model and on one public one, at each of the three depths: a picture is taken
in headless Chrome and read. The numbers of boxes and of arrows on the page are those the
function gives.

## Choices made in writing this down, for the owner to overrule

1. **A system with no parts stays a box at every depth**, and so does an app with nothing
   inside.
2. **A piece of infrastructure is a box**, inside its system or beside the systems. Routes
   through it are not drawn yet.
3. **Arrows follow ELK's routes**; they are not React Flow's own curves, which would cross
   the frames.
4. **Nothing happens on a click.** Pan and zoom with the mouse work, as React Flow gives them.
