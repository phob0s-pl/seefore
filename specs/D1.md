# D1: The input of a change

Stage D1 of `plan2.md`, the first of the four parts of milestone 4. It defines what a person
brings before a change is drafted, and gives it a schema. Two architects proposed apart
(`research/changes/input/`, out of Git), and the owner then chose a simpler form on
2026-11-24: "I have to have name, prompt and given. Simple." This page writes that down.

## What the input is

**The input of a change is what the person asks for, and what they answer when asked.** It
names no record that is not there yet: that is the change itself, the next part.

| Key | Needed | What it is | Who writes it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `prompt` | yes | What the person asked for. It may be anything: one line, a need, a full brief | The person. The assistant may put it into better words, with nothing added |
| `given` | no | What the person answered when the assistant asked: each answer one line | The assistant, from the person's answers |
| `name` | yes | What the change is called | The assistant, last, from the prompt and every answer |

    name: Listening copies on time after a donation
    prompt: >-
      Run two jobs at once. The last donation's listening copies sat behind the overnight
      integrity scan. No new hardware.
    given:
      - The copies took longer than the two working days a donation peak may take.
      - Any way that gets them out within two working days will do. Two jobs is one way.

## The three keys

- **`prompt` is the person's.** The assistant may mend its wording: a slip, an unclear
  sentence, a name of the model put right. It adds nothing: no fact, no part, no reason and
  no limit that the person did not give. It takes nothing away that changes what is asked.
  A secret typed into a prompt, such as a password or a token, is left out, and the
  assistant says that it left it out.
- **`given` holds answers only.** One line for each thing the person answered, in the
  assistant's words and true to the answer. Nothing that the prompt says already, nothing
  the assistant found by itself, and no line the person did not say. With no question
  asked there is no `given`.
- **`name` comes last.** It is written when the prompt and all answers are there, so it
  says what is wanted, not what was first typed.

## How it is got

1. **The prompt.** The person types what they want.
2. **A light check against the architecture.** The assistant reads the model for what the
   prompt is about and for what stands against it: a part that is there already, a rule of
   the team, an earlier change. It reads before it asks.
3. **Questions.** It asks what it needs and cannot read. Each answer becomes a line of
   `given`.
4. **Done.** It writes the `name`. The input is done when someone who was not there can
   read the prompt and the lines under it, and say what is wanted.

## Limits

| What | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| `name` | As on every record: one line, 1 to 100 characters |
| `prompt` | 1 to 4,000 characters, on as many lines as it needs |
| A line of `given` | One line, 1 to 300 characters |
| `given` | 1 to 10 lines, none twice |

## The schema, and where the input is written

- **`schema/changes.schema.json`**, written from the shape in `src/model/records.ts` as the
  others are. It allows the three keys and no other.
- **The input is the head of a change's own record.** What a change holds beside it, and
  where its file lies, is the next part, D2. So no folder is read with this shape yet, and
  `seefore check` says nothing of a change.

## What is out

- **What a change is, decisions, and the review**: D2, D3, D4.
- **A need written apart from the prompt, the way asked for as a key of its own, and the
  records a change is about**: the two architects proposed them, and the owner left them out.
- **The questions as they were asked, and the conversation.** Only the answers are kept.

## For the owner to approve

The three keys and who writes each are the owner's. These come from the research on
questions (`research/stage-15-22/`) and are not yet said by the owner:

| # | The choice | Proposed | The other way |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | How many questions | At most three, in one message, once, each with the answer the assistant would take | No limit. The owner's own record had 6 at the start, and 5 changed the design |
| 2 | What is never asked | What the files answer; a name or a wording; whether the assistant read the prompt rightly | Anything may be asked |
| 3 | The limits | 4,000 characters of prompt, and 10 lines of `given` of 300 each | Other numbers: these two are guesses |
| 4 | A secret in a prompt | Left out, and said | Kept as typed |

## Tests

One: the schema passes an input with and without `given`, and refuses one with no prompt,
with no name, with a key it does not know, with an empty `given`, and with a line of
`given` that runs over two lines.
