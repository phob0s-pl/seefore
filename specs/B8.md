# B8: A focus on the diagram

A stage after B7 of `plan2.md`: the two deeper depths can show one system or one app with
what it talks to. The owner's earlier tool ("arch") has it, and the owner asked on 2026-11-24
whether to do it the same way. The answer was yes, with four improvements. The owner took
three, left out the button in the click panel, and said to build it. Builder: Opus.

## What you can do after it

At the Containers depth, choose one system and see only it, opened, with everything it has
a line with as closed boxes. At the Components depth, do the same for one app. Go back to
everything with one choice, or with the back arrow.

## What is in

- **A menu "Focus" in the top bar**, beside Systems, Containers, Components, in arch's
  look. It is there at the Containers and the Components depth, when there is something to
  choose.
- **Its choices at Containers**: "All systems", then each system that has something inside
  it, by name. **At Components**: "All apps", then each app that has something inside it.
- **"All" is the default** at both depths: the diagram as it is today.
- **A focused view shows the chosen system or app as a frame**, with what is inside it as
  the whole diagram draws it at that depth.
- **Around it stand its neighbours, as closed boxes**: everything that has a line with the
  frame or with something inside it.
  - At Containers: people, pieces of infrastructure in no system, and other systems, each
    system one box.
  - At Components: the other apps and stores of the same system, each one box; people; and
    other systems, each one box. The app's own system is not drawn.
- **Only lines that touch the focus are drawn**: one end is the frame or is inside it. A
  line between two neighbours is not drawn, and a box with no line to the focus is not
  drawn. An end inside a closed box is drawn at that box.
- **The focus is in the address**, as `focus=<id>` beside the depth. A choice is a step of
  the history, so the back arrow returns to the view before. A focus the model does not
  have shows everything.
- **The focus is kept on a change of depth where it fits**: from an app at Components, the
  Containers link shows that app's system. From a system at Containers, Components shows
  everything. The Systems depth has no focus.
- **A new focus is a new diagram**: it is laid out and fitted afresh, and what was picked is
  let go. The click, its panel and the highlight work in it as they do today.
- **In `src/model/`**, with no browser in it: what can be chosen at a depth, the focus an
  address means, and the view of a focus.

## What is out

- **A button on a box or in the click panel** that opens the box one depth deeper. The owner
  left it out.
- **Zones for a focused view**, and any other change to the layout.
- **A link from a record's page** under Models to its focused diagram.
- **Any other focus**: an app at Containers, a system at Components, a store, a person, an
  owner or a technology.

## Tests, about ten

The functions, on small models written inline: what can be chosen at each depth; a focused
system holds what is inside it and closes another system to one box; a line from inside to
an app of another system ends at that system; a line between two neighbours and a box with
no line to the focus are not drawn; a focused app has its sister apps as closed boxes and
no frame of its system; a focus that is not known gives everything; an app's focus becomes
its system at Containers. In the browser: a choice in the menu puts the focus in the address
and draws fewer boxes, and the back arrow returns to everything.

## The try

On the private model and on two public ones: every choice of both menus is opened, and what
is drawn is compared with the model. The zoom at fit is noted beside that of everything. A
picture of a focused system and of a focused app is read.

## Choices

1. **No button in the click panel**, as the owner chose.
2. **"All" is the default at both depths**, and Components has it too. arch opens on one
   system and has no "all" at Components.
3. **Lines between neighbours are left out.** They are what makes a view of one system as
   crowded as the whole.
4. **At Components the app's own system is no frame.** Its sister apps stand beside the app
   as boxes; a frame round them all would be a third border to read.
