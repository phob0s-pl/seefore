# C1: What a feature is

Stage C1 of `plan2.md`: one page that says what a feature is. It defines, and builds nothing.
It comes from a study of 2026-11-24 (`research/features/`, out of Git): what the owner's
earlier tool ("arch") had, the research and the old work, answered apart by Fable and by
Astra, who agreed. The owner took their six recommendations and approved this page, and
with C2 changed one of them: a flow names one feature, always.

## What a feature is

**A feature is a thing the software does for somebody, under the name its own people already
use for it, as it is today.** The name is found, not made: a menu entry, a line on a price
list, a page of the documentation.

| Tell it from | By this |
| --- | --- |
| A flow | A flow is one path: a start, steps in order, an end. A feature has no step and no order. Support desk is a feature; a refund is a flow |
| A part | A part runs, holds data or is a kind of person. A feature is in nothing and may cross parts and teams |
| A requirement | A requirement can be broken: it says what must be true. A feature is there or it is not |
| A change | A feature says what is there now. What is planned or half built is a change |

## The record

`features/<id>.yaml`: the tenth folder, flat. One strict YAML mapping, as every record.

| Field | What it means | Needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `name` | What its users call it. As on every record | yes |
| `description` | What somebody gets from it, and who. What is known to be missing is said here too | no |
| `only` | Where or when it is there at all: `only: in the Netherlands`. Never "planned" | no |
| `owner` | The id of the owner who is responsible for the feature. It is written on the feature and never taken from its parts, which may have other owners | no |
| `touches` | Ids of parts and connections that are the feature's and that no flow of it reaches. A list with no id twice. The one new field: thirteen become fourteen | no |

```yaml
name: Sales reports
description: Promoters see how an event sells.
touches: [promoter, sales-reports, warehouse, loader]
```

- **The id is in the one space of ids.** Where a part has the word already, the feature takes
  what its users say: `sales-reporting`, where the component is `sales-reports`.

## A feature and its flows

- **A flow names its one feature**, always, in a key that C2 defines. A feature names no
  flow, so a new flow is one new file and changes no feature. A feature may be there with
  no flow.
- **A feature's parts are read from its flows.** `touches` adds what no flow reaches. Whoever
  shows a feature keeps the two apart: "from its flows" and "stated on it".

## The checks

The problems of one file (B1) hold for the new folder as for the nine. Across records:

| Rule | What must be true of a feature | Class |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `id-twice` | Its id is no other record's. "Give the feature the word its users say." | error |
| `reference-unknown` | Each id in `touches` and `owner` names a record | error |
| `reference-wrong-kind` | `touches` names a person, a system, an app, a store, a component, a piece of infrastructure or a connection. `owner` names an owner | error |
| `feature-alone`, new | Some flow names the feature, or it touches something. "Name it on a flow, or say in `touches` which parts are its own." | count |

- **An owner that only a feature names is in use**: `record-unused` does not count it.
- **No rule asks for a description, an owner or a flow**, or finds a stale `touches`.

## What the page will show, as far as its stage builds it

- **Features**, a third entry in the sidebar: one flat list by name. A row shows the name,
  its `only` and the number of its flows.
- **A feature's page**: the description, Only, Owner, its flows, and its parts in the two
  lists with their own owners. Who it is for is read from the people among its parts. On
  the diagram its parts stay bright and the rest fades, by the highlight of B7.

## What is out

| What | Why, or where it belongs |
| --- | --- |
| A tree of features | arch's own tree was there to own diagrams, which are worked out now. `in` on a feature could be added later and breaks no file |
| A best flow, a best diagram | No order of flows; a diagram is no record |
| A list of gaps | A sentence of the description. In arch 431 of 763 words were gaps, and three were left to rot |
| A state, and the changes that move a feature | `only` says where; a change says what is planned: milestone 4 |
| Requirements, acceptance lines, examples, evidence | Milestone 5, each a record that points at the feature |
| A grouping of the list, search | Milestone 7 |
| A feature named on one step of a flow | A flow has one feature (C2) |

## Choices, as the owner took them

1. **A record, not a label on flows.** A label holds no purpose, and a feature with no flow
   is not listed.
2. **The flow names its feature**: one, and always, as in arch, where a flow lay in its
   feature's folder. The owner chose this with C2, to have every flow in one place.
3. **Flat, no tree.** 4. **Five fields, no more.** 5. **`touches` now.**
6. **Reading the new folders is a stage of its own.** `plan2.md` named C3 for the page
   alone. Now C2 defines a flow, C3 reads and checks `features/` and `flows/`, and C4 puts
   both on the page.

## What is not known

- **No feature has been written in this format.** The first are the owner's own, at C3.
- **Features about data.** Two of the four in arch were about data and had no flow.
- **`touches` is written by hand and can rot.** In arch such lists were wrong within a month.
