# B11: The diagram answers the hand

A stage after B8b. The owner said on 2026-11-26 that viewing a large graph is very slow, and
asked why and whether it can be better. The page was measured as it runs, Opus searched what
is known, Fable and Astra each planned apart (out of Git), and the owner said to build the five
steps both came to, with the double click for the fourth.

## What was measured

On a public model of 513 records, in Chrome and in the system's WebKit, the engine of Safari,
which the owner reads the page in. A window of 1440 by 900 at twice the pixels.

| What | Found |
| --- | --- |
| A view appearing | 0.3 to 0.6 s in both |
| Dragging the field and turning the wheel | 60 frames a second in both, far out and close |
| The mouse going over the field, close, in Chrome | No frame over 25 ms |
| The same in WebKit | 3 to 6 frames over 25 ms in 350 moves, one of about 300 ms |
| A drag that starts on a line | Does not move the field. The reach of the lines covers 28% of the picture far out and 30 to 43% of what is in sight close |
| From the fitted view to names that can be read | Eight times closer: about 1,500 pixels of wheel, or three double clicks |
| Back to a view seen before | Laid out again, 0.36 to 0.39 s |

The frames that took long in WebKit came from one line of CSS: the fade of what is not in focus
ran over 120 ms, on some hundreds of boxes and frames at once. With that line gone and nothing
else changed, no frame was over 25 ms. The frames caused the 300 ms, the boxes the 26 to 54 ms,
and the lines none. Taking the blur of the glass away changed nothing.

## What is in

- **What is not in focus fades at once.** The fade to a quarter stays; it no longer runs over
  a moment.
- **A line takes no mouse itself.** The diagram finds the line the mouse means: the one that
  runs nearest to it within 12 of the diagram's pixels, as far as a line reached before. A box
  under the mouse comes before a line, and a line before the frame or the field it runs over.
  So the hover, the label and the click on a line are as they were, and a drag moves the field
  wherever it starts. The path that caught the mouse for each line is gone.
- **A double click brings what its first click meant near enough to read**, in a glide of
  200 ms:
  - a box comes to the middle of the room beside its panel, at zoom 0.8, or stays as close as
    the field is when that is closer;
  - a frame is fitted into that room, at most at the size it is drawn;
  - the empty field comes twice as close, about the mouse, as before, and with Shift held it
    goes twice as far;
  - on a line the field only slides clear of the panel, as after one click.
  The first click picks, as any click does. The second is the diagram's also when the panel
  that the first one opened lies under the mouse by then: the panel does not hear it. A third
  does nothing.
- **In a field too narrow to leave 240 pixels beside the panel, the whole field is the room.**
- **Where the system asks for less motion, the field comes at once**, after a double click and
  when it slides to keep a pick clear of the panel.
- **A hand that slips fewer than 4 pixels between press and release has clicked**, a line as
  a box.
- **When the field moves by itself, the hover is none until the mouse moves.** A glide after a
  double click, or the slide that keeps a pick clear of the panel, brings something else under
  a mouse that rests; what was picked would be faded for a line that happens to lie there. After
  a drag or a turn of the wheel, what the mouse means is found anew, as it lies under it still.
- **A layout is kept**, by its view, its depth and its shape, the last 24 of them, for as long
  as the page is open. A view seen before stands at once, with no "Laying out…".
- **A file of the build is kept by the browser**: what is under `assets/` has the hash of what
  it holds in its name, and is served to be kept for a year. The page, the model and everything
  else are asked for each time, as before.

## What is out

- **A layer of its own for the field, drawing only what is in sight, lines on a canvas, less
  glass, less thorough layout.** Nothing measured asks for them. Drawing only what is in sight
  would also hide a line whose ends are out of sight.
- **A button for the zoom of reading.** The double click was there already and stopped short.
- **Safari's pinch.** Nothing in React Flow hears it; what it does on the page was not seen.
- **A double tap on a touch screen.** React Flow's own zoom heard it and is off now; the page is
  read with a mouse or a trackpad.
- **A layout kept across a reload.**

## Tests, about twelve

On functions: the mouse means a line only within its reach; a layout asked for again is not
made again and is there at once, one that failed is not kept, and only the last few are; a
file of the build is served to be kept, and the page and the model are not. In the browser: a
drag from a line moves the field and picks nothing, a click there still picks the line, and so
does one by a hand that slips; after a drag the hover is on what lies where the mouse was let
go; what is not in focus has no fade over time; a double click on a line, a box, a box closer
than reading, a frame, the empty field, and the empty field with Shift; a double click on a box
that stands where its panel opens; one on a line that lies partly under its panel; less motion
and a narrow field; a view seen before stands without being laid out.

## The try

The same model and window, after:

| What | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frames over 25 ms as the mouse goes over the field in WebKit, 350 moves: the whole model at Containers | 3 to 6, one of about 300 ms | None |
| The same, the whole model at Components | 2 | None |
| The same, one system at Containers | 13 to 16 | None |
| Back to a view seen before, in WebKit | 0.36 to 0.39 s | 0.03 to 0.05 s |
| A drag that starts on a line | The field stays | The field moves |

The highlight was as busy in both: the set of faded things changed 8 or 9 times a sweep. In
Chrome nothing was slow before and nothing is after. Safari itself was not driven, since it
lets no script drive it as it is set; a hand on its trackpad was not measured.

## The review

A review of the first build, by reading, found seven things; six were so and are mended, each
with a test:

| Found | Mended by |
| --- | --- |
| After a drag the hover looked where the mouse was pressed, since no move is heard while a drag lasts | The place the mouse was let go |
| A double click on a box where its panel opens: the second click fell on the panel, and could press a button there | The double click is told by the count of clicks, brings what the first click meant, and the panel does not hear the second |
| In a narrow field a box was brought to a strip a few pixels wide, and a frame to a speck | The whole field as the room |
| A click on a line by a hand that slips was a drag | Four pixels of slip |
| Shift and a double click went closer, where it went farther before | Shift goes twice as far |
| A double click on a line under its panel stopped the slide clear of it | The slide runs again |
| The glide ran where less motion is asked for | It comes at once |

The seventh, the double tap on a touch screen, is out, as above.

## Choices

1. **The fade goes for lines too**, though only boxes and frames cost: boxes that jump beside
   lines that fade would look uneven.
2. **The reach of a line stays 12 pixels of the diagram**, not of the screen. Far out, a
   reach in pixels of the screen would lie over the whole picture.
3. **A box comes before a line under the mouse**, as it did when both took the mouse.
4. **The double click is the diagram's own**, and React Flow's is off: its zoom by two
   ran on a box too, and would run beside the glide. It is told by the count of clicks and not
   by the browser's own event, which goes to what lies under the mouse at the second click.
5. **A layout is kept by all of its view**, names too, which place nothing: a key that holds
   too much lays out once more than it must, and one that holds too little shows a stale
   picture.
