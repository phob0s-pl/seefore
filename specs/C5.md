# C5: What a step does to its store

A stage after C4, the first of six that build "data kept in a store". The owner asked for it
on 2026-11-26; Fable and Astra designed it apart, a reader reviewed it, and the owner chose
every open point (`research/data-kept/`, out of Git). Each spec was written by both apart,
and joined. Built in this order: C5, C5b, C5c, C5d, C5e, C5f, and reviewed as one. The lines
of code that a spec names are those of `main` before stage H1: a builder reads it as it is.

**After it** a writer says on a step whether it reads or writes its store, and what it takes
out. `seefore check` holds both keys and counts what is left unsaid. A connection's page and
a data record's show a step's data going the way the step says.

## The format: two keys of a step

| Key | The sentence `seefore guide flows` prints for it |
| --- | --- |
| `access`, after `data`: an old name in a new place | "Whether this step reads or writes the store its connection ends at: `read` or `write`. Only on a step whose connection says `access: read-write`; left out there, the store shows nothing of the step's `data`. A step that reads one thing and writes another is two steps." |
| `removes`, after `access`: new | "The ids of the data that is no longer in the store after this step, records in data/, none twice: on a step on a connection that ends at a store. A read that takes a job off a queue names the job in `data` and in `removes`." |
| `data`: as it is, and one sentence more | "… On a step that writes a store it names what the step puts there; on one that reads a store, what the store holds: what the reader makes of it is named on the step that puts it somewhere." |

- **Both may be left out**, so no model that is valid today stops being valid. `read-write`
  is refused on a step, as any value its key does not take. **Settled here**: they stand
  after `data`, in that order; the guide's example flow stays.

## The way of a step

A step is **at a store** when `does` names a connection whose `to` is a store. **Settled
here**: one function, `wayOf` in `src/model/model.ts`, says its way for the checks, the pages
and C5b alike. `Step` gains `access` and `removes` there.

| The connection's `access` | The step's way |
| --- | --- |
| `read` | Read |
| `write` | Write |
| `read-write` | The step's own `access`. Left out: not said |
| none | Not said |

## What follows the way

- **Which way a step's data goes**, on a connection's page and a data record's, is the step's
  way: `src/model/travel.ts` turns it by the connection alone today (line 112). **Settled
  here**: with no way said both pages show it as today, and `way-unsaid` counts the step.
- **Settled here**: a record that a step names in `removes` alone does not travel: the step is
  none of "Steps" on its page, and a team's rule that looks at `data` does not see it
  (`judge.ts` stays, and a test holds that). It is among the data of its flow's feature.
- **Settled here**: both keys join `STEP_KEYS` in `flow.ts` now: under a change a step that
  writes either otherwise is Updated. The bar says what differs from C5f on. **It touches**
  `records.ts`, `model.ts`, `rules.ts`, `counts.ts`, `travel.ts` and one line of `flow.ts` in
  `src/model/`, the schema by `npm run schema`, and the tests.

## Tests, about twelve

The schema of a flow passes a step with both keys, and refuses `access: read-write` and an id
twice in `removes`. The guide prints the two keys after `data`, and what a read names. The way
of a step, row by row. `step-shape`: either key on a reply, in a part, on a step that runs a
flow and between two apps. `removes` that names nothing, and one that names a store.
`step-access`: `access` under `write`, and under no `access`; `removes` on a read of a
database, and none on a read of a queue; one id in both lists on a write, and on a read of a
database the problem of `removes` alone. The three counts with their lines; data that only
`removes` names is in use. `rename` rewrites an id in `removes`, and `remove` refuses its
record. `access: read` on a step turns its data round on both pages. A team's rule does not
see what a step only removes. A step whose `access` changes is Updated.
