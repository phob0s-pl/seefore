# F2: The skill `review`, with its readers

Stage F2 of `plan3.md`, as the owner turned it on 2026-11-26: several readers of a change,
each with a lens, kept only where they find what a plain reader misses, and rules for which of
their points are kept. Three rounds within 75 US dollars: two on the hospital group, the third
the result on the grocery firm, held back. Fable wrote and reviewed, Opus 5.5 at medium read.
F0's two pages from the web, on techniques of design review and on public reviewers, gave the
lenses and the first list of what not to raise. They, the runs and the reviews are out of Git.

**After it** `/seefore:review <change>` runs apart from the session that drafted the change,
starts four readers, joins what they find, and writes one review of at most ten points.

## The skill and its readers

`plugin/skills/review/SKILL.md`, 100 lines, and four agents in `plugin/agents/`. A reader has a
fresh context, no shell, and writes nothing: its points come back as its answer.

| Reader | Its lens | What the rounds showed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `plain` | None: a careful architect | The baseline. Kept as a guard: it hit two seeds that no lens has read |
| `assumed` | What was decided without the person, in `assumed` or silently in the records | The one lens whose lead is beyond chance, in each round: 5, 6 and 7 known faults, where the plain reader has 1, 1 and 4 |
| `failure` | The steps of each flow the change moves: their order, their moment, their failure | Few hits of its own, and the most points worth acting on: 31 of 37 in round 1 |
| `trust` | By whose leave the data is where the change sends it | Kept narrowly: two faults in both rounds, one of them as a block by it alone |

- **Dropped after round 1**: lenses on the need, on what is written, and on whether the model
  stays whole: none hit a known fault that the plain reader missed. **One reader alone** is
  an argument, as `/seefore:review <change> assumed`.
- **A trial showed** that a skill can start readers its plugin ships, also when it runs apart.
  In two of six runs it ended on "waiting" for them; the text now tells it to wait for each.

## The rules of what to keep

Four readers bring about 23 points a change, half of them said twice, and most of them true.

| # | The rule | The count that decided it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A question to the person is kept, also when one reader alone asks it | 11 of the 16 points that hit were questions; 4 of 8 faults were raised by one reader |
| 2 | One point a subject: the same record and the same outcome | About half of the lenses' points repeat |
| 3 | What three or more readers raise ranks up, after rule 4 | 22 of 28 worth keeping |
| 4 | A finding that rests on one sentence is kept only when the sentence says it, checked for every kept point | 15 of 15 stricter readings not worth acting on; three readers shared one misreading |
| 5 | What is as true of a flow the change does not touch goes out | 6 of the 10 untrue points of round 1 |
| 6 | A number chosen inside a limit that `given` states is one note; with no limit stated the rule does not apply | 15 points in round 1; used wrongly twice in round 2, each lowering a hit |
| 7 | Data kept "without end" goes out where no line names a term | 8 of 8 |
| 8 | Stale prose is one joined note | 38 of 95 points on consistency not worth acting on |

**At most ten points: a ceiling.** In round 2 every review had exactly ten points and six
blocks, and 9 of the 17 points that the cap cut were worth acting on. Round 3 still cut at a
ceiling of six blocks, which is gone now; the text says what goes first when more are left.

## How it was tried

- **The changes** have known faults: the 16 seeds written by hand, twelve of which break one
  hidden line each, and drafts of E2, for which a second model said which lines they break.
  **A hit**: a point names the fault, or asks the question that would bring it out. A second
  model judges it; a reviewer holds its verdicts, and reads the question strictly.
- **Round 1**: the plain reader and six lenses, each alone, on eight changes of the hospital;
  **round 2**: the skill with four readers, on six; **round 3**: the grocery firm's eleven.

## What the rounds measured

Known faults hit by a block, as the reviewer holds them. In rounds 2 and 3 a reader's count is
of its own points, taken out of the runs of the skill.

| Round | Known faults | Plain | `assumed` | `failure` | `trust` | The skill's review | Its points, and those worth acting on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1, hospital, each reader alone | 18 | 1 | 5 | 3 | 2 | not run | not counted so |
| 2, hospital, the skill | 16 | 1 | 6 | 0 | 2 | 5 | 60, and 44 |
| 3, the grocery firm, held back | 13 | 4 | 7 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 98, and 70 |

| The bar, written before the first run | In round 3 | Met |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Of the six faulty seeds, four have their fault hit by a block | 5 of 6 | Yes |
| No block on a clean seed | 10 blocks, 7 of them true and worth acting on | Not measurable so: a clean seed is clean of hidden lines only |
| Half of the lines that the drafts break are hit | 1 of 7 | No |
| At most four points for each fault hit | 16.3 | No |
| The skill hits two faults more than the plain reader | 6 against 4 | Yes, with none to spare |

- **A review is worth more than its hits.** Seven points in ten are true and a person who owns
  the system would act on them, on both organisations. Of 257 points that hit nothing in
  round 1, 10 were untrue.
- **What a draft breaks mostly rests on what only a person could say**: 1 line of 7 was hit
  on the grocery firm's drafts, 5 of 14 on the hospital's. A seed's fault is easier: its
  `given` often says the line.
- **Joining costs hits.** In round 3 the four readers hit 8 faults and the review kept 6: one
  was cut at the ceiling of six blocks, one was lost inside a join. Both are mended, untried.
- **A review costs about 1.70 US dollars and four minutes**, a quarter of it each reader.

## What is not shown

- **The last three changes are untried**: no ceiling of blocks; the lines of `assumed` first;
  a join that keeps every case.
- **One run a cell.** Only `assumed` stands clear of chance; one round cannot choose between
  the plain reader and `trust`. Rule 4 is not shown to work: four blocks that rest on a
  stricter reading were kept in round 3.
- **The known faults of a draft are a second model's verdicts**, and "worth acting on" is a
  reviewer's, not the owner's: the twenty points that the plan asks the owner to mark wait.
- **Never run**: one reader alone by its argument; a second reading after a correction, which
  comes with F3. **For the owner**, from the sessions: points cannot share a cause or hang on
  one answer (11 of 11 in round 3), and a question must be a block (6).
- **Tests**: the plugin's test holds the four readers, each with no shell and no way to write.
